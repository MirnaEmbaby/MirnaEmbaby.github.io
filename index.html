Two papers on making LLM answers shorter

Master's capstone · 2026 · Supervised by Deloitte, Jun – Oct 2026 · 3-person team

MinimalLM: Verbosity-Aware Decoding for Token-Efficient LLM Inference

A training-free decoding method that makes unmodified LLMs answer in fewer tokens while keeping the answer complete. It combines a guardrail instruction, a length- and entropy-aware stop-token boost, a completeness gate and a final check.

47.6%fewer generated tokens
19–68%faster answers
0cut-off answers
8.04 / 10judge score vs 8.42 baseline

Tested on Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-3B over 330 questions from MT-Bench, AlpacaEval and Arena-Hard-Auto, with 45.6% token savings in six other languages. I built the data pipeline, experimental conditions and generation runner, co-developed the decoding mechanism and co-authored the manuscript.

Accepted · ACLing 2026 · Procedia Computer Science, Elsevier (Scopus Q2)

Beyond Truncation? Entailment-Licensed Elision and the Limits of Semantic Preservation in LLM Decoding

A follow-up that tests the simplest baseline first: cut the model's own answer to the same length. An entropy-gated controller removed 25.5–34.9% of tokens within pre-registered BERTScore margins, but its output was always a prefix of the original answer, so the saving was truncation. A survey of 23 training-free mechanism families found none that picks content better than truncation without oracle labels.

The paper then introduces ELIDE, which withholds a sentence only when an NLI model judges it entailed by the text already shown. ELIDE beats matched-length truncation on three model families. Reading 936 of its elisions showed that 59% harmed the answer, a failure no automatic measure caught. Scoping the licence to its discourse unit cut the harmful share to about one in ten.
